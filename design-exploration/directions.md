# Scribix design theses

All four directions use the same homepage content, actions, data, and functional order. They are intentionally different in composition, hierarchy, typography, color behavior, material, shape, imagery, and motion.

#### 01 — Prism Pulse

**Thesis:** A luminous creator canvas that makes AI clipping feel fast, optimistic, and immediately approachable.

- **Feels like:** energy, momentum, possibility, accessible intelligence.
- **Reference mix:** Vizard for chromatic energy and in-hero action; Figma for controlled color fields; OpusClip for source/output proof.
- **Composition and hierarchy:** centered promise → embedded upload rail → a horizontal-to-vertical transformation stage → quiet four-step footer.
- **Typography and density:** large geometric sans with very tight headline tracking; medium-low density; short technical labels only around media.
- **Color and theme:** violet is the action and selection role, amber is the output/highlight role, and neutral translucent surfaces protect legibility. Light uses a pale lavender canvas; dark uses deep indigo rather than pure black.
- **Material and shape:** luminous field, restrained translucency, large circular crop geometry, rounded upload rail.
- **Imagery and iconography:** user footage and vertical candidates are the proof; icons remain simple line forms.
- **Interaction and motion:** on entry, the source settles first and the three output clips fan in along a shared beam. Upload feedback should begin immediately; reduced motion changes this to a short opacity/state transition.
- **Signature move:** one focused light field appears to split a horizontal source into vertical outputs.
- **Strongest advantage:** closest to the Vizard quality the user already likes, while giving Scribix its own source-to-output visual device.
- **Main risk:** continuous gradients can reduce clarity or feel trend-dependent if carried unedited into the product workspace.
- **Avoid when:** the brand needs to read primarily as sober enterprise infrastructure.
- **Deliberate restraint:** no purple-on-white stock “AI sparkle” cards, no animated noise everywhere, no gradient text outside the main promise.

## 02 — Signal Cutroom

**Thesis:** A compact, video-first instrument that makes every AI decision feel inspectable and production-ready.

- **Feels like:** speed, precision, control, professional confidence.
- **Reference mix:** OpusClip for dark video staging; Linear for state hierarchy; Frame.io for creative-workspace structure.
- **Composition and hierarchy:** edge-led split screen, with the promise and upload controls on the left and the source/candidate console on the right.
- **Typography and density:** condensed uppercase headline, compact monospace state labels, higher information density.
- **Color and theme:** near-black or cool mist foundations; acid green is reserved for ready, selected, and primary action; no decorative gradient.
- **Material and shape:** flat structural panels, one-pixel rules, almost-square corners, subtle depth only under the main console.
- **Imagery and iconography:** media preview dominates; icons look like tool glyphs rather than illustrations.
- **Interaction and motion:** state-led transitions—upload, processing, ready, selected—with short linear or critically damped movement. Reduced motion retains color and label changes.
- **Signature move:** the homepage behaves like a live clipping console with a legible system status.
- **Strongest advantage:** the most coherent path from marketing site to the signed-in editor.
- **Main risk:** the density and industrial tone may intimidate first-time or lifestyle creators.
- **Avoid when:** warmth, friendliness, and broad consumer accessibility matter more than expert confidence.
- **Deliberate restraint:** no hazy ambient glow, oversized rounded pills, decorative glass, or playful illustration.

## 03 — Pop Assembly

**Thesis:** A modular, hands-on creator kit where source, clips, and workflow feel easy to rearrange and own.

- **Feels like:** playful competence, creative ownership, momentum without intimidation.
- **Reference mix:** Figma for modular color logic; CapCut for creator familiarity; Klap for single-job simplicity.
- **Composition and hierarchy:** asymmetric block board: promise/upload module on the left, source and output modules on the right, four physical-feeling workflow tiles below.
- **Typography and density:** friendly heavy sans; short line lengths; medium density with strongly separated modules.
- **Color and theme:** royal blue owns the main promise, yellow owns generated output, coral owns the primary action, and mint marks completion. Dark mode moves the neutral canvas to deep navy without changing those semantic roles.
- **Material and shape:** solid color blocks, small rotations, visible borders, offset shadows, tactile cards.
- **Imagery and iconography:** video is presented as movable material; icons are simple and bold.
- **Interaction and motion:** cards snap into position as each stage completes. Hover/touch lifts only actionable blocks; reduced motion uses border and shadow changes.
- **Signature move:** the long-video transformation reads as colorful pieces being assembled into publishable output.
- **Strongest advantage:** most approachable and brand-distinctive for independent creators.
- **Main risk:** it can feel youthful or toy-like to established media teams.
- **Avoid when:** the main audience expects a sober production or enterprise tool.
- **Deliberate restraint:** no sticker avalanche, emoji UI, rainbow text, or motion without state meaning.

## 04 — Cinema Index

**Thesis:** A cinematic publishing surface that frames every upload as valuable footage becoming a finished story.

- **Feels like:** premium craft, authorship, editorial confidence, calm focus.
- **Reference mix:** Runway for media-led restraint; Descript for authored control; VEED for progressive workflow clarity.
- **Composition and hierarchy:** one full-bleed cinematic source stage carries the promise; an indexed side rail holds source and candidates; the workflow reads like a production sequence.
- **Typography and density:** refined sans with editorial scale but without the current warm-serif identity; low-density promise paired with a compact candidate index.
- **Color and theme:** cool silver/ink in light mode and wine-black/ice in dark mode; raspberry marks action, ice blue marks generated output.
- **Material and shape:** cinematic frame, thin borders, quiet overlays, controlled blur inside the candidate rail.
- **Imagery and iconography:** excellent real user footage is essential; icons are nearly absent so the frame does the work.
- **Interaction and motion:** a slow but brief camera settle reveals the side index, then clip selection moves along the source timeline. Reduced motion swaps the settle for a static reveal.
- **Signature move:** source footage is the background story while selected clips form a precise visual index.
- **Strongest advantage:** strongest premium positioning for podcasts, studios, and content teams.
- **Main risk:** depends on consistently strong footage and can feel less instantly playful.
- **Avoid when:** the main promise must feel casual, free, and extremely lightweight.
- **Deliberate restraint:** no faux film grain, clapperboard clichés, sepia, warm parchment, or dramatic motion that delays upload.

## Theme contract shared by all directions

- Light and dark use the same semantic hierarchy: canvas, surface, elevated surface, text primary, text secondary, border, action, selection, success, warning.
- Theme changes cannot alter layout, status meaning, or the appearance of the exported video.
- Large saturated surfaces must still provide WCAG-readable text; secondary text is never communicated by opacity alone in production tokens.
- Reduced transparency replaces translucent panels with opaque semantic surfaces.
- The eventual signed-in editor should use calmer surfaces than the marketing hero, even when Prism Pulse is selected.

## Quality-gate result

- 11 current products with direct sources across all four evidence layers.
- Exactly four equal-size directions and one frozen content contract.
- Each direction differs across at least five design dimensions.
- Keyboard-visible focus, reduced-motion handling, light/dark semantics, and a full-size desktop view are represented.
- Verification gap: these are HTML/CSS design studies using simulated media, not usability-tested production screens. Responsive adaptation will be designed only after a direction is selected.
